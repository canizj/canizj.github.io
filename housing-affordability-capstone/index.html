03 / Analytical approach

Connecting the data
to the question.

Data preparationExploratory analysisVisual communication

The capstone asked what factors were associated with the increase in U.S. home prices since the 1980s. The team report, dated October 14, 2022, examined home prices, household income, mortgage rates, inventory, population, and inflation using historical datasets.

01 · Price versus income

Home-price growth and median household income increasingly diverged over the period examined, making affordability more challenging.

02 · Mortgage-rate cycles

Changing interest-rate environments affected financing costs and the ability of households to purchase homes.

03 · Financing and investors

Institutional investment patterns and shifts in financing arrangements introduced additional dynamics into housing demand and affordability.

Dataset sources

The original report identifies Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) and the U.S. Census Bureau as primary sources, with supporting research from Realtor.com Research and the National Association of Realtors. The linked report documents the original references and historical scope.

Prepare and align

The report describes standardizing column names and tabular layouts, aligning annual time periods, and using Excel formulas and PivotTables to prepare data for analysis.

Explore and explain

The team used correlation analysis, time-series charts, year-over-year changes, and housing ratios to explore relationships. I created the Tableau dashboard to bring together pricing and income trends, finance categories, investor activity, and employment-sector context.

Present the work

The report, datafolio, dashboard, and video offered different ways to follow the same analytical story. The project helped establish the analytical communication skills I later brought to teaching, operations analysis, and assessment design.

Historical project: the findings reflect a 2022 student capstone. Exploratory relationships are not proof of causation, and the analysis is not presented as a current housing-market assessment.
