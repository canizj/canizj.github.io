02 / Operations Analyst recruiting assessment

Distinguished Scholar
Selection.

A separate Operations Analyst application assessment exploring transparent criteria for recognizing approximately the top 10% of a learner cohort.

Assessment context: a November 8, 2023 recruiting invitation described this as a take-home case study simulating a realistic Operations Analyst project. The task was to set Distinguished Scholar criteria and explain the selection methodology. It was not an assignment performed while employed in that role.

The analytical question

How should a program identify top-performing fellows when academic scores cluster at the top, attendance records contain gaps, and community engagement offers an imperfect participation signal?

Methodology

My assessment narrative describes a 618-fellow cohort. The approach preserved raw data, distinguished attendance-reporting gaps from absences, flagged missing assignments, and applied academic and attendance criteria before considering engagement as a secondary selection factor.

The combined narrative covers two application iterations. The November approach reports selecting 62 fellows while preserving a tie at the engagement cutoff. The March approach describes contextual review of low engagement alongside strong academic results and attendance. These are separate iterations rather than one combined algorithm.

From selection to program insight

The analysis also examined demographic, performance, and engagement patterns using Google Sheets, Tableau Public, and Power BI. Recommendations included investigating gaps between Review Module and Extended Case performance and considering opportunities for outreach and learner support.

Interpretation and limits

Attendance adjustments and engagement thresholds are policy choices that need clear explanations. Slack activity may reflect access, communication preferences, or available time. Cohort associations can guide further investigation, but do not establish causes or marketing returns.

The recruiting email confirms the exercise’s purpose. Cohort size, selection counts, and analytical results are reported in the assessment narrative; the underlying selection workbook has not been independently checked here. No operational adoption or demonstrated fairness outcome is claimed.

03 / Flagship project

Project Ruby.

Designing assessment workflows around educator judgment.

The challenge

Extended Cases ask learners to apply practical Excel and SQL skills. Assessing those submissions requires consistent criteria, useful feedback, and room for valid alternative approaches. The process also needs clear ownership when an issue belongs to curriculum, learner support, or program operations.

My contribution

I independently recreated the rubrics for all four Extended Cases, EC1 through EC4. I also helped audit the EC1, EC2, and EC3 materials and reviewed whether the assessment process would work as intended.

The wider initiative involved collaboration with Frankie Law and Program Manager Ivan Lamas-Sanchez on grading support and curriculum quality. The project handoff also documents my work on assessment procedures, EC4 SQL review, and cross-functional workflow design.

Individual contribution

Four recreated rubrics

I authored the revised rubrics for EC1 through EC4, defining the assessment criteria that supported educator-led grading.

Collaborative contribution

Material and process audits

I helped review case materials and evaluate whether the grading and support process would achieve its intended purpose.

How the Excel grading support works

The original EC1 workbook separates diagnostic checks from TA-controlled point assignment. Formula-text and formula-presence checks help reviewers inspect a learner’s approach. Separate Boolean rubric controls feed point formulas, allowing the educator to make the scoring decision.

This structure supports a practical distinction: a diagnostic flag can prompt review without deciding the score by itself. Educator judgment remains essential when a correct answer uses an unexpected approach.

Verified in the file: separate diagnostic and scoring formulas in EC1. This was a static source inspection; native Excel control interaction and recalculation were not tested. The supplied copy does not establish an automated feedback compiler, override logger, or database synchronization.

SQL assessment and alternative solutions

The EC4 archive contains a Jupyter/SQLite learning environment, an AdventureWorks database, solution cells, and notes on alternative SQL approaches. It illustrates why assessment needs to account for equivalent solutions, including CTE and subquery variations.

The archive identifies a grader variant, but the inspected main notebook does not establish a complete automatic scoring system. Automated acceptance of all equivalent queries and live LMS integration remain unverified.

What the work contributes

Project Ruby connects assessment criteria with the operational steps around grading: review, feedback, learner support, and curriculum improvement. Its value is the combination of structured evaluation and clear process ownership. Measured time savings, error reductions, and cross-program deployment are not claimed here.

Project Ruby / Future-state design

Five lanes.
Clear ownership.

The proposed operating model maps responsibilities across the Teaching Assistant, Project Ruby Engine, Fellow, Content Team, and Business Operations. It connects submission and review with feedback, coaching, curriculum QA, and escalation.

Future-state architecture: this is a proposed operating model. Automated logging, retry enforcement, and master-database synchronization shown in the diagram are not verified deployed capabilities.

Keep learner feedback moving

A parallel route separates grade delivery from internal quality reporting, so curriculum or operational follow-up has its own owner.

Handle exceptions explicitly

The model routes deadline extensions back into submission tracking, returns incomplete grading tasks to the TA, and escalates retry and appeal exceptions.

The historical rubric and audit work provides the project context. The diagram extends that context into a future-state design; it is not evidence that every automated step was implemented.
